# ADR-001: Use Kafka Between Telemetry Collectors and Processing

## Status

Accepted - 2026-06-03

## Context

The AIOps platform receives metrics, logs, and traces from the payment service and related dependencies. At medium scale, the target workload is around 100 services, 500 GB of logs per day, and 1M metric events per second. If every OpenTelemetry Collector writes directly to Loki, VictoriaMetrics, Jaeger, and the ML feature pipeline, downstream systems become tightly coupled to producer traffic. During an incident, log volume can spike 5x exactly when engineers need the data most. Direct push also makes replay difficult: if the feature extraction job is down for 10 minutes, the anomaly detector loses training and inference data for that period.

## Decision

Place Kafka as the transport layer between OpenTelemetry Collectors and downstream consumers. Collectors publish telemetry events to Kafka topics such as `metrics.raw`, `logs.raw`, and `traces.raw`. Flink consumes those topics to compute rolling features, VictoriaMetrics and Loki ingestion workers consume at controlled rates, and a cold-storage sink writes raw events to S3 for replay and post-mortem analysis.

## Consequences

Positive consequences:

- Kafka gives replay capability, so a failed processing job can resume from offsets instead of losing data.
- Producers are decoupled from storage speed, reducing telemetry drops during incident spikes.
- Multiple consumers can read the same stream: dashboards, ML, archive, and ad-hoc analytics.
- Backpressure is easier to manage because consumers can lag temporarily without breaking services.

Negative consequences:

- Kafka adds about 5-20 ms end-to-end latency.
- The platform team must operate brokers, partitions, retention, ACLs, and monitoring.
- Monthly build cost increases by roughly $2,500 at medium scale for Kafka transport, based on the cost model in this assignment.

The extra latency is acceptable because logs and ML features do not require sub-10 ms response time. The reliability and replay benefits are more valuable for AIOps than the small latency penalty.

## Alternatives Considered

1. Direct push from collectors to storage. This is simpler and lower latency, but storage outages or traffic spikes can drop data.
2. Vector or Fluent Bit aggregator without Kafka. This reduces complexity but still lacks durable replay.
3. Managed Kinesis or Pub/Sub. This reduces operations work, but increases cloud lock-in and may be less portable across environments.
